# Closing the Peak application scope

Confirmed direction on 23 October 2026: build an interactive substation peak-demand
forecasting and response-simulation dashboard around Theo's 2021–2025 approach.
The previous 2010–2013 household experiment is retained as development history;
it is outside the remaining implementation milestones and final application.

## Purpose and audience

The application helps the project team and assessors explore one question:
can a day-ahead forecast identify high-load evenings, and how much might voluntary
household load shifting reduce those peaks under stated assumptions?
It is a research decision-support prototype, delivered as a locally runnable
Streamlit browser application with reproducible Python models and evidence.
Network-planning use is the demonstration scenario; operational deployment is
outside the four-session scope.

## Main user journey

1. Select a supported substation and historical date. Show location/zone, source
   period and data availability. Target Theo's 60 residential-type substations,
   subject to source verification and the cohort audit.
2. View the day-ahead 48-half-hour forecast, central estimate and uncertainty range.
   Highlight 16:00–21:00. In historical replay, reveal actual readings for comparison;
   they must never enter the forecast inputs.
3. Inspect the peak-event threshold, estimated probability and plain-language
   warning. Show the transparent proposed price multiplier with its 30% activation
   threshold and 1.5× cap. The multiplier is a simulated policy signal.
4. Adjust voluntary participation, movable load, exemptions and battery assumptions.
   Compare original and simulated flow curves, displaced MWh, evening peak reduction
   and any rebound after 21:00. Keep assumptions visible next to results.
5. Inspect forecast drivers and evaluation evidence, including baseline comparisons,
   uncertainty coverage, warning quality, exclusions and model-training carbon estimate.

## Application views

| View | What the user can do |
|---|---|
| Forecast and peak warning | Choose site/date, inspect forecast and uncertainty, reveal actuals, inspect warning and multiplier |
| Response scenarios | Change assumptions and compare baseline versus shifted demand, energy conservation and rebound |
| Evidence and explanations | Inspect feature importance, forecast/warning metrics, split dates, provenance and limitations |

The interface must distinguish signed measured flow from the magnitude forecasting
target when the historical sign convention requires it. Label units as MW for
power and MWh for energy. Do not present magnitude as signed net demand.

## Delivery boundaries

- Historical replay uses the 2021–2025 study and eligible validation/test dates;
  it does not imply live 2026 forecasts or a live electricity-grid connection.
- Household responses, battery effects and pricing impacts are scenarios, not
  measured customer behaviour. There is no appliance control or actual billing.
- A working local app, reproducible setup, evaluation outputs and demo guide are
  the core deliverables. Hosting, user accounts and live APIs are outside scope.
- Unverified document figures are not application results. The UI uses outputs
  generated by the integrated pipeline with clear validation/test labels.
- Required scientific checks remain part of the new substation work. The pivot
  retires the old experiment; it does not remove leakage or correctness checks.
